\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Models}
Two pretrained encoders: LaBraM-base (5.8\,M parameters, ported from the
reference release) and CBraMod (5.0\,M, taken from the community
\method{braindecode} implementation). Using the community implementation for
the second model removes the objection that a negative result reflects a faulty
port. Two models that we could not run are stated as scope rather than
omitted: \method{braindecode}'s LaBraM fixes its temporal-embedding slots and
rejects our input length, and EEGPT's interpolation target cannot be set
through its public signature.

Supervised comparators are ShallowConvNet~\cite{schirrmeister2017deep} in
narrowband and broadband variants, ATCNet~\cite{altaheri2023atcnet}, and EEG
Conformer~\cite{song2023conformer}, each tuned on validation under the same
protocol. We additionally report a \emph{seed ensemble}: the same cropped
ShallowConvNet trained at several seeds with held-out class probabilities
averaged uniformly, the seed count and the decision to ensemble both fixed on
validation. It is the strongest supervised configuration we have, included so
that the foundation models face our best comparator rather than a convenient
one.

\subsection{Adaptation modes}
Three. A \emph{frozen probe} trains a logistic head only, with regularisation
and window length selected on validation. \emph{Fine-tuning} updates encoder
and head, with learning rate, freeze depth, head warm-up and stopping epoch
selected on validation; layer-wise decay 0.9, weight decay 0.05, label
smoothing 0.1, batch 16, 60-epoch budget. Batch 16 rather than the reference 64
because a subject supplies roughly 230 fit trials, at which batch 64 yields
about four optimizer steps per epoch. A \emph{pooled leave-one-subject-out}
probe trains on eight pooled subjects and is evaluated on the ninth.

The selected fine-tuning recipes are \emph{inverted} between the two models:
LaBraM requires ten frozen blocks at learning rate $10^{-4}$, CBraMod requires
nothing frozen at $5\times10^{-4}$. Recipes must therefore be selected per
model on validation, and ``foundation model'' is not one thing.

\subsection{Controls}
Three, and most of the paper's strength is in them.

\emph{Architecture-matched random initialisation.} The identical architecture
with random weights, separating \emph{these pretrained weights carry nothing
for this task} from \emph{this architecture cannot learn from a few hundred
trials}. We verified that pretrained weights genuinely load: 178 of 179 tensors
differ from a fresh initialisation. This control is established methodology in
recent EEG-FM
audits~\cite{kontras2026neuroatlas,banville2026neuralbench,zare2026negativecontrol};
we adopt it rather than introduce it.

\emph{Matched input.} Each supervised comparator --- ShallowConvNet, ATCNet and
EEG Conformer --- retrained on the identical broadband arrays the foundation
models receive, with the temporal kernel and filter count searched per subject
on validation. Because that search runs separately for each input pipeline, the
resulting term measures the pipeline together with the architecture retuning it
requires, not the pipeline alone. This separates the model from
the preprocessing pipeline, and Section~\ref{sec:decomp} reports what it
actually does.

\emph{Stratified-split reference.} Validation and evaluation drawn from the
same session, establishing that validation ranking is informative when session
shift is absent and near-uninformative when it is present.

\subsection{Datasets}
BCI~IV-2a (9 subjects, 2 sessions, 4 classes, 22
channels)~\cite{brunner2008graz,tangermann2012competition} is primary.
BNCI2014\_004 (9 subjects, 2 classes, 3 channels: C3, Cz,
C4)~\cite{leeb2007bnci004} is the independent replication. Two caveats belong
in the open: BNCI2014\_001 was rejected as a replication because it is the same
recordings as BCI~IV-2a rebuilt, and BNCI2014\_004's three electrodes make it a
\emph{harder} setting for models pretrained on dense clinical montages rather
than a neutral one. It answers whether the deficit is specific to four classes,
not whether it holds at equal channel count.

\subsection{Metrics and statistics}
Brier score is primary, accuracy secondary, with expected calibration error
(ECE) and negative log-likelihood reported
alongside~\cite{brier1950verification,gneiting2007proper}. A single temperature
is fitted on the selection split and clamped at 20.0; we report how many
subjects saturate that clamp, since saturation is itself evidence of how
overconfident the raw logits are.

Comparisons are paired within subject and tested with a two-sided Wilcoxon
signed-rank test~\cite{wilcoxon1945individual}, corrected across the whole
reported family by Benjamini--Hochberg~\cite{benjamini1995fdr}. We also report
the \emph{attainable-$p$ floor}: the smallest two-sided exact Wilcoxon $p$ is
$2^{1-n}$, which at $n=9$ is $0.0039$. Several results below sit exactly at
that floor, meaning the design has reached its mathematical limit rather than
measured a larger effect. Throughout we write ``we could not detect'' rather
than ``there is no'', and ``in these datasets'' rather than ``in EEG
decoding''.
